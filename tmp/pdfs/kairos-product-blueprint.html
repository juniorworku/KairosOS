01 / KAIROS BLUEPRINT

Product description

Version 2.0 / 30 October 2026. Target product specification; capabilities below are planned unless separately verified in the implementation record.

Purpose

Kairos is a web-first AI executive assistant that turns goals and commitments into an agreed plan, then follows up until each item is completed, cancelled, or deliberately deferred. It serves founders, solopreneurs, freelancers, and team leads in a personal workspace; shared team administration is deferred.

Users already have calendars. Kairos earns its place by keeping a commitment ledger, detecting what needs attention, preparing briefs and drafts, and bringing concrete decisions back for approval. Its calendar-first interface connects this assistant work to available time, goals, and focus.

Product promise

Define what matters. Agree on a realistic plan. Activate follow-up loops. Work with protected focus. Kairos tracks the evidence, prepares the next useful action, and asks before changing the schedule or sending anything.

Five assistant engines

EngineResponsibilityAuthoritative inputs and outputs
Commitment ledgerTrack promises, reply debt, waiting items, meeting follow-through and VIP attentionCommitments from permitted email, supplied meeting notes and manual entry; linked tasks, resolution evidence and reviewable follow-ups
Calendar guardianDetect conflicts and overload, protect/recover focus time, propose freed-time use and travel buffersSynchronized calendar events and focus reservations; findings and proposed calendar changes
Briefing composerPrepare daily briefs, week-ahead preparation, meeting dossiers and Friday wrapsReads shared engine facts and summaries; produces a coherent brief without creating competing facts
Goal engineSupport seasons, goal clarification, GPS-style planning, weekly plans, trajectory and velocityGoals, tasks, measurable progress and recorded effort; reviewable plans and evidence-based adjustments
InsightsExplain where time went and what creates inbox noiseCalendar/session and email statistics, with coverage and freshness; descriptive reports and proposed fixes

These are logical responsibilities, not a requirement for five autonomous agents or separate deployed services. They share a single context layer and durable records. Deterministic code owns checks, ranking rules, dates and capacity calculations; bounded AI steps extract or classify new text and write drafts, summaries and coaching.

Product model

A season is an optional longer planning container. A goal defines an outcome and success measure. A plan connects milestones, tasks, time allocations and follow-up loops to that outcome. A support area holds ongoing responsibilities without an artificial goal.

A commitment records something owed, the responsible person, who expects it, its source, due date if known, and resolution evidence. Tasks are actions that fulfil commitments; they are not duplicate commitment records. Independent personal tasks remain supported. A calendar block reserves time; a focus session records effort. Task completion, commitment resolution and goal progress are separate decisions and evidence.

Planning horizons are one day, one week, two weeks, four weeks, six weeks and twelve weeks. Longer plans do not reserve every future hour by default. Daily coaching preserves accepted blocks and proposes changes only when constraints or user priorities change. Users can mark a block as fixed; conflicting work is surfaced for a decision rather than silently moving it.

Loops and authority

A loop is an instance of a fixed, versioned, typed template with selected declarative parameters. Its specification defines trigger types, scope filters, deterministic checks, permitted action types, an authority ceiling, context recipe, model tiers, run/token caps and a stopping condition. Chat fills in that specification and presents what runs, when and why for confirmation. Parameters can lower authority but cannot exceed the template ceiling. Unsupported requests receive an explanation and the nearest supported option; the miss is recorded for product review.

The six starter template families are Daily brief, Daily check-out, Goal check-in, Overdue and slipping work, Focus rescue, and Weekly review. Friday wrap and week-ahead prep are modes of Weekly review. Daily coach sync is the interactive planning conversation that uses the brief; it is not a seventh ambiguous background template. Specialized assistant engine checks and output profiles must be mapped to the fixed catalog before release; see the feature catalog for the proposed mapping and unresolved boundary.

Free-text preferences may personalize output wording, length and order. They cannot alter checks, triggers, permissions or executable actions. Tasks, drafts and calendar actions inferred by the assistant are presented for approval; reading, deterministic analysis and briefing generation within granted access can run in the background. Correspondence and follow-up messages require exact per-message approval. Activating a notification loop authorizes its scheduled push/email delivery within configured limits. Every assistant-proposed schedule adjustment requires approval.

First-release boundary and build order

The first public release must deliver a full executive-assistant experience across the five engines, including commitments from email, supplied meeting material and manual entry. Calendar/goal/focus-only milestones are internal increments, not the released product.

Build Google Calendar reading, synchronization and approved publication with goals and tasks first; add meeting ingestion and follow-through next; then email ingestion and assistant workflows. Complete and verify the integrated assistant before first-release acceptance. Gmail is the proposed initial mail provider; supplied notes/transcripts are the first meeting input. Confirm import channel details before implementation; additional providers remain later work.

The web app comes first. It includes the calendar workspace, coaching, assistant workflows, in-app floating timer, optional lo-fi audio and Chrome website blocking. Desktop companions for macOS, Windows and Linux follow, including a system-floating timer where supported. Later system-wide website blocking starts with one selected desktop operating system. Native application blocking and cross-device blocking are excluded promises. The device owns session expiry and unblock recovery.

Background operation and cost

Server-side loops run even when the UI is closed. Each template defines a catch-up policy for missed runs. In-app results are the record of truth; web push and email fallback follow delivery preferences, permission and failure rules. Notification tiers are quiet, scheduled, nudge and urgent, with quiet hours and a daily limit.

Sources synchronize once into the shared store. Rolling summaries and content hashes support incremental processing; routines query the store instead of independently rereading providers. Context packs include bounded structured facts and source references. Usage is recorded per run and purpose, with reserved daily-brief capacity, per-run/per-loop caps and a global daily stop mechanism. When AI budget is constrained, fall back to clearly dated cached summaries or a deterministic digest and visibly pause lower-priority AI work.

Success measures

Prioritize commitment resolution, useful follow-up acceptance, timely reply handling, accurate and ready-before-open briefs, and reduced missed obligations. Also track time to first approved plan, goal outcome check-ins, session completion, proposal edit/rejection, notification dismissals and AI cost per purpose. Separate calendar reservations, recorded effort, task completion and measured outcomes. Set pilot targets after baseline usage; collect only consented telemetry.

Boundaries

Keep the interface minimal: calendar as the main workspace; contextual panels and drawers instead of a page for every routine. Use original Kairos copy and identity. Avoid invented progress, unexplained scores and automatic rescheduling. Broader connectors, native audio capture and AI handoff require separate capability and consent validation; they are not prerequisites for importing meeting notes into the first release.

Approval interpretation

A report/brief, deterministic finding or in-app preview is not an external mutation. Candidate extraction can persist suggestions in the ledger without accepting them as obligations. A generated reply can be displayed automatically as preview; approving creation of an external mailbox draft does not approve sending it. Direct explicit task creation needs no redundant generic approval. Every assistant schedule adjustment still needs review, with a single batch approval permitted only when all effects and destinations are listed.

End-to-end workflows

1. Connect and clarify

1. Choose plan a goal or organize existing work; select a day/1/2/4/6/12-week horizon.

2. Connect Google Calendar, then Gmail when ready, with separate source/AI/write consent. Enter availability manually if not connected.

3. Define success, path, capacity, constraints, follow-up and authority. Follow-up questions adapt to missing information; save/resume is available.

4. Show bounded import scope, progress, partial coverage and source freshness. Do not imply older history was checked.

5. Propose milestones, tasks, blocks and workflows; expose assumptions/capacity gaps. Review exact effects and destinations, accept/edit/reject.

6. Open the calendar-first workspace. Unapproved items remain suggestions; publication is approved explicitly, possibly within the same enumerated review.

2. Activate a fixed workflow

Choose one of six template families, answer a short chat, inspect the typed form preview and confirm. Chat selects only registered check/output profiles and allowed parameter values; it cannot invent new checks, code, tools or permissions. Style instructions go only to writing. Unsupported requests offer the nearest template and record a redacted catalog miss. Preview shows next run, source coverage, authority ceiling and stop condition. Changes to behavior are reviewed as a new version.

3. Morning brief and daily coach sync

The server creates a Daily brief before the app is opened. Today shows it in the coach panel beside the calendar. The interactive daily coach sync uses that report; it is not a separate automation template. Coach asks about changed capacity, energy and core priorities. Preserve the accepted weekly plan and fixed blocks. Propose only justified additions/differences, with reasons. Review and approve individual changes or an exact batch, then start focus. Skip sync without losing the existing plan.

4. Capture and resolve commitments

Email, meeting notes or manual input supplies evidence. Extract candidate commitments and match people/threads with provenance. User accepts, edits, dismisses or merges candidates before inferred work enters the accepted ledger. A commitment records what is owed; tasks describe actions that fulfill it. One task can contribute to an obligation without proving that obligation resolved.

Resolution rules:

Task: explicit user completion or a separately approved, verifiable completion action.

Waiting on them: a response creates evidence; mark resolved only when deterministic evidence is conclusive under the configured rule, otherwise request confirmation. Acknowledgments and partial answers do not close it.

Promise/meeting action: completion evidence must satisfy the promised deliverable, with owner confirmation when uncertain.

Goal: record the success measure; effort and task count are not substitutes.

Deferred work: capture reason, resume date or review trigger; pause follow-ups until that trigger. Reopen when evidence invalidates an earlier resolution and show why.

5. Guardian and approval review

A source event or time-based check identifies conflict, freed capacity, missing link, travel need or threatened focus. Focus rescue merges preventive Focus guard and reactive Focus defense. Calendar sentinel remains a set of guardian checks, not a duplicate routine.

Produce a bundled proposal with before/after, dependency ordering, source versions and exact destinations. Fixed blocks remain protected; surface a decision rather than moving them. Preserve the currently observed external calendar: approval governs Kairos's corrective action, not whether a third-party edit already happened.

Revalidate on approval. Supersede old alternatives; changed facts require renewed review. Remember dismissal signatures (reason, affected items, meaningful constraints) until those facts change. Applying one change cannot silently apply a dependent unapproved change. Report partial results and reconcile unknown external writes before retrying.

6. Focus

Start a task/block, confirm objective/duration and configured Chrome website rules. Optional audio starts through user playback controls. Dedicated view and floating in-app timer show active time, pause/resume/end and actual browser protection status. Pause releases session blocking by default; resuming restores permitted rules. Emergency unblock and local expiry remain available without network.

On finish, record accomplishment, explicit task status, remaining work and optional outcome/self-rating. Timer expiry never completes a task. Sleep, restart and abandoned sessions need a declared recovery policy before implementation; do not claim idle/sleep time as focused work. A continuation block is a new proposal. Later desktop timer companions do not imply native-app or cross-device blocking.

7. Daily check-out and weekly review

Daily check-out reconciles completed, unfinished, waiting and blocked items; `/done` opens this session. Updates explicitly entered by the user apply as entered; inferred new tasks, mailbox drafts and calendar changes remain proposals. Missing the check-out does not mark all unfinished work failed or rolled forward.

Weekly review has Friday-wrap and week-ahead modes over one evidence snapshot/review record. Friday summarizes outcomes and open decisions; week-ahead proposes next week. Deduplicate overlapping reports; preserve differences when constraints change. Compare scheduled time, recorded active time and coverage, not invented actual activity. User decides carry-forward, defer, drop or revise.

8. Closed-app operation and catch-up

Ingestion, checks, briefing and approved notifications run server-side while the browser is closed. The device independently ends website rules. Feed stores report and decision history; push/email provide permitted delivery, not guaranteed proof the user read it. On reconnect, show latest useful outputs and partial-source warnings rather than replaying every old notification. Detailed catch-up and delivery rules are in Automation.

State contracts

Plan: draft → proposed → active → completed/archived; prior accepted revision remains authoritative until changed.

Commitment: candidate → accepted → active/waiting/deferred → fulfilled/cancelled. Candidate can be dismissed; deferred needs a resume trigger. Tasks have independent todo/in-progress/done/cancelled state and scheduling links. Goal outcome state is separate.

Proposal: pending → applying → applied/partially-applied/failed; pending can be dismissed/superseded/expired/conflicted. Retries retain operation identity. Changed effects create a new review version.

Loop: draft → active ↔ paused → stopped. Run: queued/running → success/partial/failed/skipped/cancelled. Check pause/version before delivery; cancelled work cannot emit newly generated actions.

Focus: idle → running ↔ paused → ended; local end/escape is authoritative for device rule release. Reconciled timestamps must not duplicate duration or extend expired protection.

Interface architecture

Clean Tech visual system

White and light-gray surfaces, deep charcoal typography, ample white space and subtle borders. One vibrant deep-purple accent (#6438E8) supports primary buttons, links, selection and focus. A pale tint of the same accent is permitted. Neutral labels/icons/outline patterns distinguish categories, sources and statuses; no multi-color pastel category palette. Original Kairos text and assets follow the supplied reference's hierarchy and simplicity.

Six views, one calendar-first entry

ViewRole
TodayDefault entry: calendar-first shell anchored to today, with morning brief/daily coaching and urgent decisions. No metric-heavy replacement dashboard.
PlanSame shell for week-ahead work, full calendar, tasks, proposals and capacity. Preserve date/selection when switching context.
CommitmentsAccepted/candidate obligations, replies owed, waiting, promises and linked tasks; inspect evidence and resolution.
GoalsSeasons, goal setup, milestones, plans and trajectory; task scheduling returns to the same calendar.
ReviewDaily check-out, Friday wrap/week-ahead modes, outcome and coverage-aware effort insights.
AssistantTemplate setup through short chat, workflow settings/history, preferences, usage and connected sources.

Workspace composition

Coach closed: left goals/tasks/support panel approximately 26%, calendar approximately 74%, with practical min/max widths. Coach open: protect a minimum usable calendar and collapse the left panel or show the coach as an overlay at laptop widths. At an illustrative 1440 px viewport: 24 px outside margins each, 272 px sidebar, 760 px calendar, 312 px coach and two 24 px gutters sum to 1440 px. This is a prototype example, not a universal breakpoint rule. An overlay is preferable when a seven-day grid becomes unreadable.

Left panel has quick capture, expandable goals/tasks, support areas, filters and sources. Center has date navigation, day/week switch, all-day row, hourly grid, now marker, source freshness and proposal review strip. Right panel holds the brief, interactive daily coaching, follow-ups, approvals and chat. A compact primary navigation avoids adding another oversized sidebar.

Interaction rules

Drag tasks to schedule; offer equivalent keyboard/form controls. Inspect event details in a popover/drawer. Direct edits expose local/external effects. Assistant additions use dashed outlines, moves show before/after, and proposed removals remain marked until approved. Keep this time fixed is visible on blocks and respected by planning. Third-party changes display as observed facts; corrective changes require approval.

Proposals bundle related changes, show dependencies and destinations, suppress equivalent dismissed suggestions and replace obsolete versions. A concise card expands for evidence and detailed effects. Approval is specific to a version; chat is not a general grant of authority.

Focus

Dedicated task-centered view with optional audio, in-app timer and Chrome website-rule status. No native-app or cross-device protection indicators. Device owns expiry and escape. Later desktop companions supply timer access on macOS/Windows/Linux; later system website blocking starts with one supported OS. All device capabilities are labeled accurately.

Responsive and accessible

Desktop prioritizes the calendar; medium screens use drawers; phone uses day/agenda and task/coach sheets. Preserve selected date and task. Provide keyboard alternatives, focus restoration, text zoom and reduced motion. Status uses words/icons as well as styling. Loading, empty, partial, stale, denied, offline, failed and budget-limited states are explicit. See screen specifications for components and step-by-step flows.

Screen design & components

Design version: 2.0 / 30 October 2026. Clean Tech is the single visual direction. This revision includes full assistant workflows, approval hygiene and web-first website-only focus controls. Dimensions below are proposed design targets to validate in a prototype.

1. Visual system

White #FFFFFF surfaces; light gray #F6F7F9 canvas; charcoal #17191F primary text; slate #596170 secondary text; subtle #E2E5EB borders. Deep purple #6438E8 is the sole vibrant accent for primary actions, selected controls, links and focus indicators. Pale purple #F0EBFF is a tint of that same accent. Use neutral labels and outlines for event categories, not additional hues. Success, warning and error states use explicit text and icons so meaning never depends on color.

Use a clean sans-serif typeface, 32-40 px major headings, 20-24 px screen headings, 14-16 px body and 12-13 px supporting metadata. Minimum sizes and contrast require device verification. Use an 8 px spacing rhythm with 4 px fine adjustments; 16-24 px panel padding, 8 px event corners and 12 px panel corners. Borders supply separation; shadows are reserved for floating surfaces. One primary action per decision context.

2. Screen S01 / Welcome and context

Purpose: establish the user's intent without forcing a long setup interview.

Layout: slim brand header, centered content column, progress indicator and a visible Save and exit action. Present two starting choices: plan a goal, or organize existing work. Explain what the assistant will prepare. Google Calendar and Gmail connection follow with separate permissions; manual availability and supplied meeting notes remain available. Declining connections shows reduced assistant coverage honestly.

Steps: choose intent; select planning horizon; describe the desired outcome; connect sources or continue manually. Next advances only after required information is supplied. Retain entries when navigating back. Connection denial explains what remains usable.

Components: intent cards, horizon selector, text field, source connector, progress stepper, inline validation and primary Continue button.

3. Screen S02 / Goal clarification

Purpose: turn a broad ambition into an actionable brief.

Layout: structured goal form with a compact conversational follow-up area. Show known answers as editable fields, not buried only in a transcript. Ask for success measure, current position, deadline, available time, priority, constraints and dependencies. Distinguish required answers from optional coaching context.

Steps: capture goal; ask the most relevant unanswered question; summarize assumptions; let user correct; generate the proposed plan. Do not require every possible question for a simple one-day task. AI unavailable state offers a manual goal and task form.

Components: outcome fields, date picker, capacity input, editable answer chips, question card, draft-save status and Generate plan action.

4. Screen S03 / Plan review and activation

Purpose: make the assistant's proposal concrete and reviewable.

Layout: outcome summary above milestone/task list; calendar preview alongside on wide screens; proposed loops below. Each item shows purpose, estimate, date or frequency, and assumptions. A capacity summary explains unused time or overcommitment.

Steps: inspect the plan; expand supporting reasons; edit tasks or timing; choose proposed items; review listed effects; approve. External publication names the calendar destination and effects explicitly. Keep local acceptance and remote publication distinct unless a single approval explicitly includes both.

No capacity: offer reduce scope, extend deadline or change availability. Rejected suggestions leave accepted work intact. Changed availability invalidates affected suggestions and asks for renewed review.

Components: milestone list, task editor, capacity summary, calendar preview, loop settings card and approval summary.

5. Screen S04 / Calendar home

Purpose: manage priorities and real-world time in one workspace.

Desktop: goals/tasks on the left, calendar in the center and collapsible coach on the right. With coach closed, use approximately 26% / 74%; constrain sidebar to a usable range. At 1440 px, an illustrative open layout is 272 px sidebar, 760 px calendar, 312 px coach, two 24 px gutters and 24 px outer margins on each side, totaling 1440 px. Collapse the sidebar or overlay the coach when the calendar becomes too narrow. This is a prototype target, not a fixed rule.

Left: quick capture; expandable season goals with tasks; support areas; calendar/source filters. Center: date navigation, Day/Week, all-day row, hourly grid, current-time marker, source freshness and proposal strip. Right: today's sync, pending decisions, follow-ups and contextual chat.

Steps: expand a goal; drag a task into time or use Schedule; inspect event; edit through popover; start focus. Dragging assistant-proposed changes does not bypass proposal review. Direct user edits identify any external save effects. Clicking a blank slot creates an explicit task/event/block choice.

Five- or seven-day display is a user preference; default seven-day week follows the reference. Work-hour display can be expanded. Source labels distinguish Google events, local events and goal blocks without a category rainbow. Keep this time fixed is visible on blocks; routine proposals respect it. Show third-party calendar edits as observed facts immediately, then offer corrective proposals if needed.

6. Screen S05 / Daily coach sync

Purpose: agree on today's core work before focus.

Panel structure: concise daily brief; today's constraints; proposed top priorities; proposed time blocks; pending decisions; chat input. Start with actual goals and available time. Ask about changed capacity and blockers. Explain priority tradeoffs briefly, with evidence links available on demand.

Steps: review the precomputed server brief; correct priorities; tell coach today's capacity; preserve accepted blocks and fixed times; inspect only justified proposed differences; approve individual changes or the enumerated batch; start selected task. Skipping sync preserves the existing plan. The assistant does not interpret a conversational acknowledgment as blanket authorization for unspecified changes.

Components: brief card, priority list, capacity prompt, proposal cards, evidence disclosure and Start focus action.

7. Screen S06 / Proposal review

Purpose: approve every assistant-proposed schedule adjustment.

Layout: compact summary strip in calendar; detailed drawer for review. Each change shows action type, previous state, proposed state, reason, affected task, source and destination. Additions use a dashed outline; moves show old/new positions with labels; removals remain visible until approved.

Steps: select a proposal; inspect before/after; edit or dismiss; approve; revalidate current calendar; apply; report local and external outcomes separately. A stale proposal shows the conflict and offers a refreshed version. Failure must preserve accepted state or explain any partial effects.

Components: change card, before/after comparison, dependencies, selection checkbox, destination summary, Apply selected, conflict banner and retry/reconcile action. Repeated approval is idempotent. Bundle related changes, supersede obsolete alternatives and remember equivalent dismissals until relevant facts change. Task adoption and external mailbox draft creation are reviewable effects; an internal generated preview is not an accepted task or sent message.

8. Screen S07 / Focus workstation and floating timer

Purpose: protect attention while keeping essential controls accessible.

Workstation: task title and intended outcome, timer, pause/end controls, Chrome website-blocking status and optional audio. Do not show native-app or cross-device protection. Hide nonessential calendar and chat content during focus, while allowing intentional return to planning. Starting a session uses only configured supported blocking rules.

Floating timer: compact neutral surface with purple active indicator, task label, elapsed/remaining time and pause/end. Drag handle, minimize and keyboard move controls; restore a safe position on viewport change. The first scope is in-app floating. Desktop companions with floating timers follow the web release on macOS, Windows and Linux; one-OS system website blocking is a separate later feature, not native-app blocking.

Steps: select task; confirm duration/objective; start; verify supported Chrome/profile website-rule status; optionally play audio; pause/resume; finish. Proposed pause behavior releases blocking while paused. Show unavailable controls honestly. Emergency unblock works even when the server cannot be reached; timed rules expire independently.

Audio controls: play/pause, volume, track/source and mute. No automatic sound before a user gesture. Audio failure leaves the timer usable. Licensing/source selection remains a delivery decision.

9. Screen S08 / Session completion

Purpose: record evidence and choose a useful next step.

Layout: short completion sheet with actual recorded duration; accomplishment prompt; explicit task status; optional outcome measure and optional self-rating. Do not infer task completion from timer expiry. Do not show a precise focus score without defined observation inputs and validation.

Approval and resolution

Bundle related changes into one review with dependencies. Replace obsolete proposals and remember dismissals until relevant facts change. Incremental summaries and freshness

Hash content with owner/source identity and normalization; re-extract only changed material.
