Product description
Version 2.0 / 30 October 2026. Target product specification; capabilities below are planned unless separately
verified in the implementation record.


Purpose
Kairos is a web-first AI executive assistant that turns goals and commitments into an agreed plan, then follows up
until each item is completed, cancelled, or deliberately deferred. It serves founders, solopreneurs, freelancers,
and team leads in a personal workspace; shared team administration is deferred.

Users already have calendars. Kairos earns its place by keeping a commitment ledger, detecting what needs
attention, preparing briefs and drafts, and bringing concrete decisions back for approval. Its calendar-first
interface connects this assistant work to available time, goals, and focus.


Product promise
Define what matters. Agree on a realistic plan. Activate follow-up loops. Work with protected focus. Kairos tracks
the evidence, prepares the next useful action, and asks before changing the schedule or sending anything.

Product model
A season is an optional longer planning container. A goal defines an outcome and success measure. A plan
connects milestones, tasks, time allocations and follow-up loops to that outcome. A support area holds ongoing
responsibilities without an artificial goal.

A commitment records something owed, the responsible person, who expects it, its source, due date if known,
and resolution evidence. Tasks are actions that fulfil commitments; they are not duplicate commitment records.
Independent personal tasks remain supported. A calendar block reserves time; a focus session records effort.
Task completion, commitment resolution and goal progress are separate decisions and evidence.

Planning horizons are one day, one week, two weeks, four weeks, six weeks and twelve weeks. Longer plans do
not reserve every future hour by default. Daily coaching preserves accepted blocks and proposes changes only
when constraints or user priorities change. Users can mark a block as fixed; conflicting work is surfaced for a
decision rather than silently moving it.


Loops and authority
A loop is an instance of a fixed, versioned, typed template with selected declarative parameters. Its specification
defines trigger types, scope filters, deterministic checks, permitted action types, an authority ceiling, context
recipe, model tiers, run/token caps and a stopping condition. Chat fills in that specification and presents what
runs, when and why for confirmation. Parameters can lower authority but cannot exceed the template ceiling.
Unsupported requests receive an explanation and the nearest supported option; the miss is recorded for product
review.

The six starter template families are Daily brief, Daily check-out, Goal check-in, Overdue and slipping work,
Focus rescue, and Weekly review. Friday wrap and week-ahead prep are modes of Weekly review. Daily coach
sync is the interactive planning conversation that uses the brief; it is not a seventh ambiguous background
template. Specialized assistant engine checks and output profiles must be mapped to the fixed catalog before
release; see the feature catalog for the proposed mapping and unresolved boundary.

Free-text preferences may personalize output wording, length and order. They cannot alter checks, triggers,
permissions or executable actions. Tasks, drafts and calendar actions inferred by the assistant are presented for
approval; reading, deterministic analysis and briefing generation within granted access can run in the
background. Correspondence and follow-up messages require exact per-message approval. Activating a
notification loop authorizes its scheduled push/email delivery within configured limits. Every assistant-proposed
schedule adjustment requires approval.


First-release boundary and build order
The first public release must deliver a full executive-assistant experience across the five engines, including
commitments from email, supplied meeting material and manual entry. Calendar/goal/focus-only milestones are
internal increments, not the released product.

Build Google Calendar reading, synchronization and approved publication with goals and tasks first; add meeting
ingestion and follow-through next; then email ingestion and assistant workflows. Complete and verify the
integrated assistant before first-release acceptance. Gmail is the proposed initial mail provider; supplied
notes/transcripts are the first meeting input. Confirm import channel details before implementation; additional
providers remain later work.

The web app comes first. It includes the calendar workspace, coaching, assistant workflows, in-app floating
timer, optional lo-fi audio and Chrome website blocking. Desktop companions for macOS, Windows and Linux
follow, including a system-floating timer where supported. Later system-wide website blocking starts with one
selected desktop operating system. Native application blocking and cross-device blocking are excluded
promises. The device owns session expiry and unblock recovery.


Background operation and cost
Server-side loops run even when the UI is closed. Each template defines a catch-up policy for missed runs. In-
app results are the record of truth; web push and email fallback follow delivery preferences, permission and
failure rules. Notification tiers are quiet, scheduled, nudge and urgent, with quiet hours and a daily limit.

Sources synchronize once into the shared store. Rolling summaries and content hashes support incremental
processing; routines query the store instead of independently rereading providers. Context packs include
bounded structured facts and source references. Usage is recorded per run and purpose, with reserved daily-
brief capacity, per-run/per-loop caps and a global daily stop mechanism. When AI budget is constrained, fall back
to clearly dated cached summaries or a deterministic digest and visibly pause lower-priority AI work.


Success measures
Prioritize commitment resolution, useful follow-up acceptance, timely reply handling, accurate and ready-before-
open briefs, and reduced missed obligations. Also track time to first approved plan, goal outcome check-ins,
session completion, proposal edit/rejection, notification dismissals and AI cost per purpose. Separate calendar
reservations, recorded effort, task completion and measured outcomes. Set pilot targets after baseline usage;
collect only consented telemetry.


Boundaries
Keep the interface minimal: calendar as the main workspace; contextual panels and drawers instead of a page
for every routine. Use original Kairos copy and identity. Avoid invented progress, unexplained scores and
automatic rescheduling. Broader connectors, native audio capture and AI handoff require separate capability and
consent validation; they are not prerequisites for importing meeting notes into the first release.

Approval interpretation
A report/brief, deterministic finding or in-app preview is not an external mutation. Candidate extraction can persist
suggestions in the ledger without accepting them as obligations. A generated reply can be displayed
automatically as preview; approving creation of an external mailbox draft does not approve sending it. Direct
explicit task creation needs no redundant generic approval. Every assistant schedule adjustment still needs
review, with a single batch approval permitted only when all effects and destinations are listed.

End-to-end workflows
1. Connect and clarify
  1. Choose plan a goal or organize existing work; select a day/1/2/4/6/12-week horizon.

  2. Connect Google Calendar, then Gmail when ready, with separate source/AI/write consent. Enter availability
  manually if not connected.

  3. Define success, path, capacity, constraints, follow-up and authority. Follow-up questions adapt to missing
  information; save/resume is available.

  4. Show bounded import scope, progress, partial coverage and source freshness. Do not imply older history
  was checked.

  5. Propose milestones, tasks, blocks and workflows; expose assumptions/capacity gaps. Review exact effects
  and destinations, accept/edit/reject.

  6. Open the calendar-first workspace. Unapproved items remain suggestions; publication is approved
  explicitly, possibly within the same enumerated review.


2. Activate a fixed workflow
Choose one of six template families, answer a short chat, inspect the typed form preview and confirm. Chat
selects only registered check/output profiles and allowed parameter values; it cannot invent new checks, code,
tools or permissions. Style instructions go only to writing. Unsupported requests offer the nearest template and
record a redacted catalog miss. Preview shows next run, source coverage, authority ceiling and stop condition.
Changes to behavior are reviewed as a new version.


3. Morning brief and daily coach sync
The server creates a Daily brief before the app is opened. Today shows it in the coach panel beside the calendar.
The interactive daily coach sync uses that report; it is not a separate automation template. Coach asks about
changed capacity, energy and core priorities. Preserve the accepted weekly plan and fixed blocks. Propose only
justified additions/differences, with reasons. Review and approve individual changes or an exact batch, then start
focus. Skip sync without losing the existing plan.


4. Capture and resolve commitments
Email, meeting notes or manual input supplies evidence. Extract candidate commitments and match
people/threads with provenance. User accepts, edits, dismisses or merges candidates before inferred work
enters the accepted ledger. A commitment records what is owed; tasks describe actions that fulfill it. One task
can contribute to an obligation without proving that obligation resolved.

Resolution rules:

• Task: explicit user completion or a separately approved, verifiable completion action.




KAIROS / PRODUCT BLUEPRINT                                                                                         11
• Waiting on them: a response creates evidence; mark resolved only when deterministic evidence is conclusive
  under the configured rule, otherwise request confirmation. Acknowledgments and partial answers do not close
  it.

• Promise/meeting action: completion evidence must satisfy the promised deliverable, with owner confirmation
  when uncertain.

• Goal: record the success measure; effort and task count are not substitutes.

• Deferred work: capture reason, resume date or review trigger; pause follow-ups until that trigger. Reopen
  when evidence invalidates an earlier resolution and show why.


5. Guardian and approval review
A source event or time-based check identifies conflict, freed capacity, missing link, travel need or threatened
focus. Focus rescue merges preventive Focus guard and reactive Focus defense. Calendar sentinel remains a
set of guardian checks, not a duplicate routine.

Produce a bundled proposal with before/after, dependency ordering, source versions and exact destinations.
Fixed blocks remain protected; surface a decision rather than moving them. Preserve the currently observed
external calendar: approval governs Kairos's corrective action, not whether a third-party edit already happened.

Revalidate on approval. Supersede old alternatives; changed facts require renewed review. Remember dismissal
signatures (reason, affected items, meaningful constraints) until those facts change. Applying one change cannot
silently apply a dependent unapproved change. Report partial results and reconcile unknown external writes
before retrying.


6. Focus
Start a task/block, confirm objective/duration and configured Chrome website rules. Optional audio starts through
user playback controls. Dedicated view and floating in-app timer show active time, pause/resume/end and actual
browser protection status. Pause releases session blocking by default; resuming restores permitted rules.
Emergency unblock and local expiry remain available without network.

On finish, record accomplishment, explicit task status, remaining work and optional outcome/self-rating. Timer
expiry never completes a task. Sleep, restart and abandoned sessions need a declared recovery policy before
implementation; do not claim idle/sleep time as focused work. A continuation block is a new proposal. Later
desktop timer companions do not imply native-app or cross-device blocking.


7. Daily check-out and weekly review
Daily check-out reconciles completed, unfinished, waiting and blocked items; `/done` opens this session.
Updates explicitly entered by the user apply as entered; inferred new tasks, mailbox drafts and calendar changes
remain proposals. Missing the check-out does not mark all unfinished work failed or rolled forward.

Weekly review has Friday-wrap and week-ahead modes over one evidence snapshot/review record. Friday
summarizes outcomes and open decisions; week-ahead proposes next week. Deduplicate overlapping reports;
preserve differences when constraints change. Compare scheduled time, recorded active time and coverage, not
invented actual activity. User decides carry-forward, defer, drop or revise.




KAIROS / PRODUCT BLUEPRINT                                                                                         12
8. Closed-app operation and catch-up
Ingestion, checks, briefing and approved notifications run server-side while the browser is closed. The device
independently ends website rules. Feed stores report and decision history; push/email provide permitted delivery,
not guaranteed proof the user read it. On reconnect, show latest useful outputs and partial-source warnings
rather than replaying every old notification. Detailed catch-up and delivery rules are in Automation.


State contracts
Plan: draft → proposed → active → completed/archived; prior accepted revision remains authoritative until
changed.

Commitment: candidate → accepted → active/waiting/deferred → fulfilled/cancelled. Candidate can be
dismissed; deferred needs a resume trigger. Tasks have independent todo/in-progress/done/cancelled state and
scheduling links. Goal outcome state is separate.

Proposal: pending → applying → applied/partially-applied/failed; pending can be
dismissed/superseded/expired/conflicted. Retries retain operation identity. Changed effects create a new review
version.

Loop: draft → active ↔ paused → stopped. Run: queued/running → success/partial/failed/skipped/cancelled.
Check pause/version before delivery; cancelled work cannot emit newly generated actions.

Focus: idle → running ↔ paused → ended; local end/escape is authoritative for device rule release. Reconciled
timestamps must not duplicate duration or extend expired protection.

Clean Tech visual system
White and light-gray surfaces, deep charcoal typography, ample white space and subtle borders. One vibrant
deep-purple accent (#6438E8) supports primary buttons, links, selection and focus. A pale tint of the same
accent is permitted. Neutral labels/icons/outline patterns distinguish categories, sources and statuses; no multi-
color pastel category palette. Original Kairos text and assets follow the supplied reference's hierarchy and
simplicity.

Interaction rules
Drag tasks to schedule; offer equivalent keyboard/form controls. Inspect event details in a popover/drawer. Direct
edits expose local/external effects. Assistant additions use dashed outlines, moves show before/after, and
proposed removals remain marked until approved. Keep this time fixed is visible on blocks and respected by
planning. Third-party changes display as observed facts; corrective changes require approval.

Proposals bundle related changes, show dependencies and destinations, suppress equivalent dismissed
suggestions and replace obsolete versions. A concise card expands for evidence and detailed effects. Approval
is specific to a version; chat is not a general grant of authority.


Focus
Dedicated task-centered view with optional audio, in-app timer and Chrome website-rule status. No native-app or
cross-device protection indicators. Device owns expiry and escape. Later desktop companions supply timer
access on macOS/Windows/Linux; later system website blocking starts with one supported OS. All device
capabilities are labeled accurately.


Responsive and accessible
Desktop prioritizes the calendar; medium screens use drawers; phone uses day/agenda and task/coach sheets.
Preserve selected date and task. Provide keyboard alternatives, focus restoration, text zoom and reduced
motion. Status uses words/icons as well as styling. Loading, empty, partial, stale, denied, offline, failed and
budget-limited states are explicit. See screen specifications for components and step-by-step flows.

Screen design & components
Design version: 2.0 / 30 October 2026. Clean Tech is the single visual direction. This revision includes full
assistant workflows, approval hygiene and web-first website-only focus controls. Dimensions below are proposed
design targets to validate in a prototype.


1. Visual system
White #FFFFFF surfaces; light gray #F6F7F9 canvas; charcoal #17191F primary text; slate #596170 secondary
text; subtle #E2E5EB borders. Deep purple #6438E8 is the sole vibrant accent for primary actions, selected
controls, links and focus indicators. Pale purple #F0EBFF is a tint of that same accent. Use neutral labels and
outlines for event categories, not additional hues. Success, warning and error states use explicit text and icons
so meaning never depends on color.

Use a clean sans-serif typeface, 32-40 px major headings, 20-24 px screen headings, 14-16 px body and 12-13
px supporting metadata. Minimum sizes and contrast require device verification. Use an 8 px spacing rhythm
with 4 px fine adjustments; 16-24 px panel padding, 8 px event corners and 12 px panel corners. Borders supply
separation; shadows are reserved for floating surfaces. One primary action per decision context.


2. Screen S01 / Welcome and context
Purpose: establish the user's intent without forcing a long setup interview.

Layout: slim brand header, centered content column, progress indicator and a visible Save and exit action.
Present two starting choices: plan a goal, or organize existing work. Explain what the assistant will prepare.
Google Calendar and Gmail connection follow with separate permissions; manual availability and supplied
meeting notes remain available. Declining connections shows reduced assistant coverage honestly.

Steps: choose intent; select planning horizon; describe the desired outcome; connect sources or continue
manually. Next advances only after required information is supplied. Retain entries when navigating back.
Connection denial explains what remains usable.

Components: intent cards, horizon selector, text field, source connector, progress stepper, inline validation and
primary Continue button.


3. Screen S02 / Goal clarification
Purpose: turn a broad ambition into an actionable brief.

Template and loop contract
Every template defines its ID/version, supported trigger kinds (schedule, source event, threshold), scope filters,
deterministic check library, allowed output/action types (brief, notification, draft, proposal), authority ceiling, stop
condition, catch-up policy, context recipe, model tier per AI step and token/step/tool-call caps. Each loop stores
template ID/version and validated chosen values. Template upgrades cannot silently change user authority or
behavior; changed behavior needs a reviewable migration/confirmation policy.

Chat chooses a supported template/profile and fills typed fields. A confirmation preview names what runs, when,
which sources it reads, what it may prepare and when it stops. Values can reduce the authority ceiling, never
raise it. Unsupported requests show the nearest supported option and log the miss. Writing preferences such as
brief length and tone affect prose only, not trigger/check/action logic.


Approval and resolution
Bundle related changes into one review with dependencies. Replace obsolete proposals and remember
dismissals until relevant facts change. Explicit approval covers the exact listed task, draft or calendar effects;
correspondence never sends without exact per-message approval. Activated notifications use standing delivery
authorization within their configured limits. External calendar publication identifies its destination. Accepted
weekly blocks remain the baseline; new constraints or user priorities justify proposed adjustments. A fixed-time
block is not moved by the assistant.

Commitments close using type-appropriate evidence. A personal task requires explicit completion; an incoming
reply is evidence of a response, not proof that the request was resolved. Ambiguous meeting and email
conclusions require confirmation. Goal completion follows its defined success measure. Reminders, elapsed time
and sending a message do not automatically close the underlying obligation.

Shared context & AI budgets
One ingestion layer
Source adapters sync Calendar changes, Gmail history and supplied meeting notes into an owner-scoped store
of people, threads, meetings, commitments, goals, summaries and evidence references. Routines query this
store, not providers directly. Calendar cursors and Gmail history IDs are opaque provider state with explicit
recovery on expiry. A connector may require fetching changed records; incremental does not mean zero network
traffic.

First connections use declared bounded backfill (proposed mail starting point: ten days, configurable before
consent). Show coverage dates and unknown older promises; do not present bounded history as a complete
account. Resync after cursor expiry has its own bounded recovery policy and must preserve accepted local
commitments and provenance.


Incremental summaries and freshness
Hash content with owner/source identity and normalization; re-extract only changed material. Version rolling
thread/person/goal summaries with source IDs, source versions, model/prompt version and as-of time. Source
edits, deletion, permission changes and retention invalidate relevant summaries/context packs. A language-
model summary is derived evidence, not ground truth; retrieve specific source evidence for uncertain or
consequential claims.

Time passing is an input. Due dates, waiting ages, current local day, meeting lead time, resolved status and
capacity are re-evaluated deterministically even when source content hashes are unchanged. Never reuse
yesterday's brief as today's factual analysis. If nothing relevant changed, create a short fresh digest with
refreshed time-sensitive fields; reuse only still-valid prose fragments, clearly dated.

Context packs and bounded orchestration
Each template profile defines a context recipe, permitted fields, evidence IDs, freshness/coverage requirements,
model tier, maximum tokens and maximum steps/tool calls. The orchestrator builds one compact structured pack
per logical step/snapshot; sub-steps share its facts and pass typed results, not repeated transcript dumps. Do
not reuse packs across users or beyond consent boundaries. Multi-agent orchestration is optional
implementation machinery, not a requirement to run several agents for every routine.

Separate trusted template instructions from untrusted source text and free-text preferences. Output style
preferences cannot change criteria or authority. Stable profile/preferences prefixes may use provider caching
where supported; measure actual cache use rather than assuming savings. Raw credentials, unnecessary
source content and secret-bearing fields are excluded.


Budgets and run ledger
Per-user monthly allocation splits scheduled loops, extraction, drafting and chat, with a reserved daily-brief share
inaccessible to chat. Add per-loop/run token/step/tool caps, concurrency limits, and a global daily AI kill switch.
Conservative numeric limits must be configured before the first pilot; tune after one week of measured use rather
than leaving execution uncapped while collecting data.

Reserve maximum allowed usage transactionally before work; reconcile actual known usage after completion.
Unknown/cancelled provider usage retains a bounded reservation until reconciliation/expiry policy resolves it.
Track model ID, tier, prompt version, input/output/cached tokens, measured/estimated/unknown usage,
currency/pricing version, cost estimate, purpose, loop/run IDs and time. Distinguish application allowance from
provider invoice cost. Price snapshots and unavailable pricing are explicit.

Usage meter shows purpose breakdown and one proposed 80% threshold alert per budget period (exact
threshold configurable). Reserved briefing capacity is finite: if exhausted, deliver a current deterministic digest
rather than claiming AI briefing remains guaranteed.


Degradation ladder
  1. Full permitted output within reservation.

  2. Use still-valid cached summaries; no new AI analysis; refresh deterministic facts and show coverage/as-of
  limitations.

  3. Fresh deterministic digest, without generated prose, retaining pending decisions and source status.

  4. Pause lower-priority AI steps/loops and disclose what is paused. Continue essential deterministic checks
  where possible.

No budget mode bypasses approvals, stale-source protections or device-local release. New items that lack
extraction remain visibly unprocessed; existing accepted commitments are still tracked. Failures never get
silently summarized as no urgent work.


Privacy lifecycle
Before first release define source-specific retention, user deletion/export, disconnect behavior, encryption,
model-sharing consent and audit minimization. Removal/permission revocation propagates to raw content,
summaries, pack caches and embeddings if used. Retain only minimal non-content audit data permitted by the

Existing foundation and scope distinction
Repository documentation describes React/Vite/TypeScript, FullCalendar, FastAPI/Pydantic, PostgreSQL,
Celery/Redis, goals/tasks, recurrence, Google integration, proposal review, focus logging, coaching and a
Chrome extension. Reuse after targeted assessment, not a rewrite. Existing documented tests do not establish

A legacy Linux helper in this repository suspends configured executables; it is outside the newly agreed product
scope. The new roadmap excludes native-app blocking. Do not claim a hosts-file helper already exists or silently
ship legacy app suspension as the new feature. This documentation pass does not remove or alter existing code.


Logical modules
Five domain engines share ingestion/context, proposal/approval, background scheduling and delivery
infrastructure. Engines are ownership boundaries in the design, not five required microservices. Keep one
maintainable modular application unless scaling evidence justifies separation.

Shared ingestion updates owner-scoped source records and rolling summaries. Commitment reconciliation
produces candidates and evidence links. Guardian validates scheduling deterministically. Briefing composes
engine outputs. Goal engine tracks outcomes/plans. Insights aggregates coverage-aware statistics. AI sees
minimal authorized context packs and cannot execute effects. Template registry constrains trigger/check/output
combinations and authority.

Stage 1/2 prototypes may be reviewed locally, but must not be presented as the requested full release. Meeting
notes import satisfies the first release; bot-free recording is not a hidden dependency. Google Calendar is

Consistency and execution
Calendar source events are observations, not proposals: display already-made external changes promptly, then
propose Kairos corrections. Protect fixed blocks. Recheck owner, consent, proposal version, source freshness,
current availability and dependencies before apply. Persist local intent and use a durable outbox or equivalent for
external effects; no distributed transaction is implied. Stable keys and reconciliation prevent duplicate/unknown
writes from being blindly retried.

Use server scheduler/worker jobs with leases and dedup, bounded retries and pause/version checks before
delivery. Device owns session expiry and emergency unblock. Browser shutdown does not stop server loops and
server failure does not prolong website rules. AI-off mode and budget kill switch preserve manual functionality
and current deterministic findings.


Acceptance and pilot
Trace every requirement to implementation issue and evidence. Test permission/ownership, unsupported
template values, authority escalation, source injection, stale approvals, fixed blocks, dismissal suppression,
dependent batch changes, cursor expiry, missed runs, DST/time progression, duplicate delivery, cost reservation
races and device recovery. Use labeled examples to measure extraction, unresolved-vs-answered distinctions
and grounding, not just schema validity.

Before pilot configure retention and numerical budgets, performance dataset/budgets, notification limits and
supported Chrome/OS versions. Measure real usage for at least the proposed first week before tuning caps.
Production release additionally needs applicable provider verification, operational monitoring, backup/restore,
migration rollback, consent/privacy review and a separate deployment authorization. No tests, deployment or live
provider changes are claimed by this documentation update.


Verified platform dependencies
Google Calendar sync uses paginated changes and invalidated-token recovery. Calendar push requires a public
HTTPS receiver; notifications signal the need to synchronize rather than carrying the complete changed event.
See Calendar push. Polling can support local validation; closed-app production automation requires hosted
workers/scheduler and monitoring, subject to later deployment authorization.

Gmail sync requires history-expiry recovery. Gmail push requires Pub/Sub and renewable watches; configure
renewal and polling/recovery fallback. Gmail scope documentation identifies restricted scopes and applicable
verification/security-assessment dependencies for server-handled restricted data; validate audience, scopes and
exceptions before setting a public launch date.

Chrome declarative request rules apply within supported browser/profile capability, not native-app control. Web
push overview explains subscription/service-worker delivery; server jobs remain authoritative. Document Picture-
in-Picture may be an optional browser enhancement but does not replace the later cross-platform desktop
companion contract.

Technical references checked 30 October 2026. These constraints inform implementation planning, not a claim
that new connectors or desktop packages already exist.

Assessment and proposed resolutions
The user's two workflow lists are reconciled provisionally as five engines implementing domain capabilities and
six template families exposing fixed typed profiles. Specialized routines are not removed. Exact catalog mapping
is the remaining product question asked during this revision; Automation explicitly labels the draft mapping
pending confirmation.

Gmail is the proposed initial mail provider, consistent with Calendar's Google foundation and the requested build
order. Meetings initially use supplied notes/transcripts rather than native capture. Native capture, other
calendar/mail providers, relationship-cooling/Going cold and AI handoff are later additions unless separately
prioritized.

The legacy Linux implementation actually suspends apps; it is not a hosts helper. New product scope excludes
that capability. Existing code is neither silently removed nor reclassified as shipped website blocking.

Today's coaching is interactive use of Daily brief, not a seventh automation template. A generated report or
preview can appear automatically; accepting an inferred task or applying an external draft/calendar/send effect
needs scoped authority. Calendar external edits are observed facts, not retroactively gated by approval.
